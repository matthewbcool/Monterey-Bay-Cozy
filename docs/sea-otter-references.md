# Southern sea otter references

Research and visual inspection: **November 2, 2026**. Target species: **southern sea otter, *Enhydra lutris nereis***. The [U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service species account](https://www.fws.gov/species/southern-sea-otter-enhydra-lutris-nereis) establishes the California subspecies. The [Monterey Bay Aquarium account](https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/animals-the-ocean/animals-a-to-z/sea-otter) confirms its resident otters are southern sea otters and describes their local coastal habitat.

This is an original procedural **Objects-library and Tank asset** with float, swim, groom and dive poses. Its Tank route alternates surface rest and grooming with dives and submerged swimming. No new wild population, Gym placement or sighting is implied. The [root art direction](../README.md#art-direction-painterly-cel-shading) and [shared LOD contract](lod.md) govern the model. Photographs establish anatomy and pose; they do not require photoreal fur, PBR reflections or a separate quality tier.

## Shape and motion priorities

Preserve a connected elongated chest and abdomen with a round, broad head; a short muzzle with two subdued pale cheek pads; a prominent dark nose; tiny round ears; compact articulated forepaws; and **two distinct broad, webbed hind feet plus a separate flattened, tapered tail**. Keep the tail visible in the extended form rather than reducing it to a stub. Exact submerged surfaces and joint centers are inferred, and should remain simple. The hidden anatomy does not justify added fine detail.

- **Float:** belly upward, head slightly raised and forepaws resting near the chest. Use restrained body roll and occasional independent foot adjustments; avoid continuous full-body thrashing.
- **Swim:** the Tank cruising pose is belly-down with the forepaws tucked close to the torso; this is an authored choice rather than a claim that all underwater travel is prone. Aquarium of the Pacific describes underwater propulsion through the hindquarters, hind feet and tail moving up and down. Use a gentle rear-body wave and foot sweep, not a fish-like lateral tail beat or dog-paddling front flippers.
- **Groom:** alternate small paw-to-muzzle/cheek/chest strokes, with occasional torso curl and hind-foot adjustment. The NOAA sequence supports independent hands and interrupted action. Keep the paws from passing through the nose, eyes, belly or each other.
- **Dive:** change the internal body attitude toward a streamlined, head-first pose, then use the swimming articulation. The host owns actual depth and travel; a library pose must not unexpectedly translate the public placement root. The inspected images do not establish a full dive cycle, depth or speed.

Motion rates, amplitudes, blending, surface bobbing and dive timing are authored animation choices, not measured physiology. These references support movement type and body relationships. They do not establish universal swimming speeds or exact stroke frequencies.

## Shared style and LOD review

Use broad dark-brown body planes, a warmer/lighter face and throat, restrained muzzle contrast and small dark eyes/nose. Fur is a broad color and silhouette treatment: omit strands, glossy wet-fur reflections, modeled teeth and dense whisker geometry. Any Near-only whisker accent must earn its cost and disappear cleanly at Mid/Far. Pale facial color varies between animals; it is not a mandatory pure-white mask.

Near should spend geometry on the head, connected limb joints and readable webbed feet. Mid should simplify contours and facial accents while preserving the two rear feet and separate tail. Far should retain the elongated floating body, head and rear silhouette without adding draws. All levels must preserve the same rig, metre scale, placement, seeded identity and physics anchors. Use the existing toon material and shared LOD owner; actual triangle/draw counts and both-backend screenshots belong in the implementation review after measurement.

For visual review, compare the model to the **whole-body** and **face/paw** screenshots above at a common pose. A dark sphere with a pale face is insufficient: the small hands, large hind feet, substantial separate tail and connected elongated trunk establish sea-otter identity.

## Tank behavior timing

The second pass uses long belly-up surface rests, intermittent grooming, a shorter head-first descent, tucked-paw underwater cruising and a smooth ascent/roll back to floating. The exact cycle lengths, surface share, depths and speeds are authored for this small demonstration. They are not measurements of the photographed animals. Surface contact is an animation/path convention, not simulated buoyancy or a husbandry plan.

# Lovers Point Testing Gym: geography and scene assumptions

The Testing Gym is a playable exercise world around Lovers Point in Pacific Grove, with a coarse Monterey Bay regional view. It combines the project’s existing OpenStreetMap coastline/building download with an October 22 extract of classified routes, beaches, the pool and volleyball court. Everything is generated with TypeScript; no aerial photo, external model or downloaded image texture is loaded by the gym. Shader lookup/contact fields are generated locally.

**Menu → Monterey Bay overview** frames the 37.5 × 53.4 km cached region north-up. **Bay place** focuses a sourced city/place point for visual inspection; it does not teleport the avatar. The bay map is a separate LOD-owned scene root. Near the park it is culled, and the existing local scene remains active; the full-bay view instead hides detailed local roots and reveals a flat fill beneath their map cutouts. The local rippled water patch is bounded near the studied coast, leaving the regional ocean to represent the rest of the bay without covering inland terrain. The Otter Man suit can fly across the regional bounds using cached land elevation and coarse seafloor samples. These are approximate reference surfaces, not new walking paths, boat routes, authored town collisions or navigation-grade bathymetry. Source provenance and resolution are in [the regional map guide](monterey-bay-region.md).

## Sources and coordinate frame

- **OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0:** local `src/data/monterey-map.json`, downloaded October 13, 2026. The bounded derived data is `src/data/lovers-point-gym.json`: 117 coastline vertices, 77 contiguous path/road runs and 25 building footprints. The dataset retains the Beach House's OSM feature ID `way/224525165`. Show [OpenStreetMap attribution](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) with the gym.
- **OpenStreetMap detail extract, October 22, 2026:** [bounded map API response](https://api.openstreetmap.org/api/0.6/map?bbox=-121.9185,36.6245,-121.914,36.6275), derived in `src/data/lovers-point-features.json`. Twelve polygons retain OSM IDs: five parking areas, breakwater `way/931232625`, pool `way/350647473`, volleyball `way/350647475`, beaches `way/633871936`, `way/633871946`, `way/633972335`, and park `relation/16861626`. The park relation is a boundary, not evidence of continuous grass. The 86 clipped route runs retain highway classification and source tags. Ocean View Boulevard is a secondary asphalt road; footways, paths and six stair routes are separately classified. Most widths are authored; footway `way/486665218` retains its cached `width=2` metre tag. Surface tags now distinguish asphalt, paving and unpaved routes.
- **City of Pacific Grove, Lovers Point Park Facilities assessment:** [official PDF](https://files.cityofpacificgrove.org/Document_Center/Departments/Public%20Works/Buildings%20%26%20Grounds/Lovers%20Point%20Park.pdf?t=202510301223500), report July 7, 2025, site visit June 16, 2025. PDF pages 1, 33–34, 44 and 53 were visually inspected on October 22, 2026. Photos inform the granite retaining walls, dark pool railings, sand court, exposed rocky point, spreading cypress crowns and the Beach House’s grey-green exterior, cream window bands, three front gables and ochre awnings. Page 44 contains an aerial site plan (credited to Google by the report), inspected as a reference only. The document is marked draft. Photos are references only and are not redistributed as game textures.

- **USGS 3DEP terrain, downloaded October 22, 2026:** cached `src/data/lovers-point-elevation.json`, from the [USGS elevation service](https://elevation.nationalmap.gov/arcgis/rest/services/3DEPElevation/ImageServer). Its source metadata identifies the `CA_AZ_FEMA_R9_Lidar_2017_D18` project, NAVD88 vertical datum and 1 m source terrain. The game uses a resampled 60 × 59 grid at approximately 5 × 6.23 m spacing. This is terrain guidance, not survey accuracy, bathymetry or a live tide reading.

Origin is WGS84 longitude **−121.91685**, latitude **36.6256**. The same local equirectangular convention as the map study projects longitude east into +X, latitude north into −Z, with +Y up and one unit per metre. Earth radius is 6,371,008.8 m. Local extracted coordinates are rounded to the nearest millimetre for stable storage; this rounding does not establish survey accuracy.

The original 117-vertex coastline and 25 building footprints remain intact. The new route segments are clipped to X −145…150 m, Z −160…130 m instead of dropping crossing segments. Polygons retain complete closed rings; the park multipolygon joins its original outer ways. The reproducible, standard-library-only extractor is `scripts/extract-lovers-point-features.py`:

```sh
python3 scripts/extract-lovers-point-features.py /path/to/osm-detail.xml --downloaded 2026-10-22 --output src/data/lovers-point-features.json
```

Download the linked XML separately when intentionally refreshing map data; the running gym never contacts OpenStreetMap or loads photos. Keep the source URL/date and ODbL attribution with derived geometry. This is a stylized, map-informed place, not a survey replica.

## Authored geometry and gameplay

The offshore bottom uses a separate [USGS bathymetry crop](bathymetry.md), with explicit shoreline and NoData approximations. The park follows the cached USGS elevation grid in metres. Ground colors, routes and markings are clipped into disjoint regions on one surface, then triangulated on the same DEM planes used by the height sampler. There are no stacked ground decals. The static display water level is an authored 1 m NAVD88 reference, not a current tide. Sharp walls, stair dimensions, nearshore joins, tree dimensions, roof shapes and structure heights remain authored approximations. Beach, pool and court footprints now come from the map. Untagged footpaths default to 1.85 m, step routes to 1.5 m, secondary roads to 6.4 m and other roads to 4.5 m. The tagged asphalt footway uses 2 m. East and south beach stairs use a photo-informed 2.1 m width; the east beach connection tapers to 1.5 m. These stair widths are estimates. The 18 × 9 m court lines are an authored inset within the mapped sand apron. The pool basin remains decorative; its surrounding deck is accessible through the opening gate. Fence and leaf collision follow their narrow visible footprints. See the [October 25 recreation pass](recreation-area.md). The Beach House wall height is 6 m with an authored 5.5 m roof rise and 1.7–2 m front gables; the Grill and pier utility hut use October 26 photo estimates of 2.45 m and 2.3 m walls; remaining background masses are 3.5 m. These heights are authored for this stylized study. Roofs are clipped to mapped outlines; windows and awnings follow actual facade segments. Granite retaining walls and borders have gaps for shore access; their height and placement are photo interpretation. Building collision uses conservative bounding boxes, so some irregular footprints have extra clearance.

The inland play boundary is an authored crop, while the sea boundary follows the mapped coastline. A 28 cm avatar collision radius is checked against boundary segments, box proxies and circle proxies. This prevents leaving the shore and entering scenery. The controller follows terrain and authored access surfaces and rejects abrupt height changes. A kinematic jump can clear low terrain ledges but not drops or collision proxies (see [jumping](avatar-creator.md#jumping)); swimming has its own [water rules](avatar-creator.md#swimming-and-breath). There is no rigid-body or multiplayer solver. Speeds are animation/game choices: **1.7 m/s walk, 3.5 m/s run**.

Spawn is X 25 m, Z −35 m, with root Y sampled from the terrain plus 0.045 m clearance. Five original exercise types remain along the shoreline: tackle box, fishing rod, docked rowboat, casting station and bat star observation; the Otter Man station and dive boat extend the current activities. These are explicit gameplay placements, not locations of those objects in the real park. Boats and equipment retain their library scale. The existing habitat bat star is observed, not collected. The invented ground markers and connecting dirt exercise paths have been removed; the mapped paved network remains.

The controller owns the avatar's placement and yaw; the rig owns walking, blinking, outfit deformation and emotes. Equipping the rod calls `setRodEquipped(true)` to remove its old collision proxy. The caller owns attaching it to the hand and returning or disposing it before disposing the world. The former spawn-bench interaction is removed. Ground Sit remains available through the emote wheel.

## Rendering and lifecycle

The complete park (including library objects, excluding avatar/sky/output pass) is checked against **38 draws / 69,472 Near triangles** (64,000 for park assets plus 5,472 for six instanced monarchs) (raised on October 23 from 32 / 60,000 for the dive boat, scuba locker and neighbourhood backdrop; see [real-world data](lovers-point-real-world-data.md#budget-change)), with instance counts included. Terrain-conforming ground, routes and access surfaces cost more geometry than the initial flat study. The browser review (historical authoring evidence omitted from this public snapshot) records dated, source-hashed Near/Mid/Far counts, unique typed-array storage and actual camera-culled submissions separately. The live rendering panel reports the currently loaded scene. Backend-added default attributes can increase recorded storage after rendering; these totals are not GPU-memory measurements.

The [rocky-reef dive site](gym-dive-site.md) is a separate root and budget (**44 draws / 80,000 Near triangles**), hidden and paused beyond 85 m. Its library fish, jellies, attached animals, anemones and instanced kelp are excluded from the park ceiling above. Kelp fronds reach the water surface and sway through a vertex offset; shared underwater caustics use two procedural texture samples with no extra scene draw. Distance LOD changes rendered geometry, while fish routes, attachment contacts, collision and world identity remain independent of detail selection.

`src/viewer/testingGym/` separates geometry helpers, source-based buildings, pool/court landmarks, shoreline/access surfaces, terrain sampling, instanced nature and spatial batching. Trees reuse one library prototype per species in 256 m cells centred on the park; all 52 park inventory trees fit five draws. Granite uses 96 m cells. Each instance keeps its own matrix and tint; LOD uses the largest projected **individual** in its batch. Furniture/walls and buildings use 128 m spatial batches. Ground and paths share broad terrain-conforming batches; water uses one inexpensive grid. These large planar/building batches may stay Near in Auto; no world-streaming or per-instance occlusion is claimed.

Land assets use shared matte toon materials; the bay uses the existing cross-backend TSL water. The world combines broad vertex-painted color patches and restrained water glints with a procedural weather sky, astronomical sun/moon lighting and visibility fog. Terrain and scenery opt out of rim lighting. There are **no photo textures, new postprocessing, outlines or reflection captures**. Three blended PCF sun cascades cover the camera frustum to 3,600 m (clamped to its far plane), with detailed near coverage, light-space texel stabilization and resolution-aware bias. Base uses three 1024² maps and Standard three 2048² maps. The far cascade fades beyond local clear-weather fog; walking and flight use the same policy, removing the visible player-centred cutoff. Shadow submissions and storage are additional scene costs: two extra shadow passes compared with the former local map. See current coverage verification (historical authoring evidence omitted from this public snapshot). The seed remains **6256**. The shared TSL ripple-water shader animates from one time uniform; static instances upload their matrices once. The gym uses a 421-triangle coastline-clipped water surface with `forceSinglePass`, no refraction capture, and the open-water profile rather than the harbor’s local bathymetry. Fine ripples come from fragment shading, not dense geometry. The mapped shore uses the shared animated swash ribbon described below. The October 23 ocean look pass (historical authoring evidence omitted from this public snapshot) uses the supplied video for turquoise color, broad lighting swells, broken crests and a speed-driven rowboat wake. These are fragment-shader effects; physical wave heights and the boat buoyancy sampler are unchanged. The October 26 [water optics pass](ocean-water.md) replaces camera-distance color with a 48 KiB lookup of the existing seafloor; absorption and colors remain artistic. It also replaces broad regular bands and hard glints with filtered irregular ripples and soft, weather-aware highlights.

The weather query point is **36.6265° N, 121.9166° W** inside the park; it does not replace the geometry origin. **Menu → Sky & weather** starts with current weather and the actual clock, offers clear/scattered/overcast/fog/rain previews and a Pacific date/time preview, and labels nearby observations, regional forecasts, stale values and missing fields. NWS discovery and observations/forecasts refresh on entry and every 15 minutes while the Gym is active and visible; leaving cancels pending requests. The client's ten-minute throttle bounds ordinary repeated entry, and intentional cancellation permits an immediate retry. Clouds and rain are visual interpretations of regional data, not sensors at the park. Rain is a last-hour observed or forecast-interval average; probability is separate. See [weather provenance and astronomy limits](weather-sources.md).

Sun and Moon directions, lunar illumination and sidereal rotation are calculated from UTC at the park coordinates. The star field is seeded artwork, not a star catalogue. Display disc radii are intentionally enlarged (sun .008 radians, Moon .011 radians); cloud geometry, apparent coverage and fog appearance are stylized. The atmosphere adds a 528-triangle sky plus at most 160 / 240 rain quads on Base / Standard (320 / 480 triangles), up to two draws, no image textures and no shadow or postprocessing passes. Those costs sit outside the park-only budget above. The full-screen procedural shader's pixel cost remains relevant even when terrain LOD reduces geometry. Reduced motion freezes decorative cloud movement and hides rain streaks; underwater view hides the atmosphere and uses the existing underwater fog. Lighting changes are scoped to the Gym, including its water shading; water level, physical waves and boat buoyancy keep their authored contracts.

The **Park overview / Walk view** control changes the camera and suppresses nearby rain streaks in overview. The **World & rendering notes** panel exposes Auto/Near/Mid/Far, all-level world counts and unique geometry storage. All colliders, source raycasts, interaction ranges and instance slots remain fixed across LODs. The shared controller registers the full gym before display. Disposal releases instance resources plus all canonical/derived geometry and materials exactly once; returning an equipped rod remains the caller’s responsibility.

`tests/testing-gym.test.ts` covers finite/index-safe geometry, all-level contracts, unchanged instance buffers, connected walking routes to every station, style/budget ceilings and teardown. `tests/testing-gym-browser.mjs` records both graphics backends, real submitted counters, forced/automatic LOD, overview controls, walking/landmark views and phone layouts. `tests/avatar-browser.mjs` exercises actual keyboard/touch movement, sitting, equipping and returning the rod. Desktop touch emulation is not a physical-phone performance result.

### Light shoreline foam

The October 26 foam pass replaces the fixed pale coastal strips with a narrow procedural swash ribbon. Its broken, soft edge advances about 2.2 m toward the mapped shore and retreats on an approximately seven-second cycle; a faint wash fades behind it. Phases vary along the shore. This is photo-inspired artistic motion, not calibrated tides or surf physics. The ribbon follows low beach terrain, stays at water level beside tall banks, and shares the water wave height, light level and clock. Reduced motion and paused Gym animation therefore hold the same foam phase. It is hidden from underwater views.

`src/viewer/testingGym/shoreFoam.ts` uses 380 Near triangles in one transparent, single-pass draw, with no textures, particles, extra render targets or per-frame geometry rebuilds. It joins the park's existing LOD and disposal ownership. `tests/shore-foam.test.ts` checks the geometry contract; `tests/shore-foam-browser.mjs` captures four phases and reads isolated foam pixels on WebGPU and WebGL 2. Captures are in `artifacts/shore-foam/`.
